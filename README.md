REBOUND
Game Pitch — “Bounce Back”
 Pitch
Hey everyone, my game is named Rebound. Rebound is a really fast-paced 2D trampoline-ricochet game where players are constantly falling but have an opportunity to select their ricochet from any given trampoline.
Players have to be an explorer who is trapped inside a collapsing tower. Jumping is not possible at all and flying also cannot help at this point. There is one and only solution: landing on the trampolines, freezing the time for a while and choosing the trajectory for the next leap.
However, there is another trick – launching straight up is not allowed. In order to move further, players will have to utilise ceilings, walls, moving trampolines and other obstacles in order to change the course of the fall.
How It Works
When you land on a trampoline, time briefly slows down. A launch arc appears, showing where you’ll go based on your aim and your current momentum.
You move the mouse to choose your rebound angle, then click or release to launch. The faster you are falling, the more force you have—but that also makes the launch harder to control.
So the trampolines aren’t just springs. They’re aiming tools. You’re not just jumping upward; you’re reading your momentum, planning a trajectory, and using the environment to keep moving.
The Gameplay Loop
You start near the bottom of a collapsing tower. The camera slowly rises, so you’re always under pressure to keep moving upward. If you fall below the camera, you lose.
As you fall, you steer slightly in the air and look for the next trampoline. When you land, time slows for a fraction of a second. You aim your rebound, launch, and keep going.
The tower is made of short hand-designed sections. Some routes are safe but slow. Others need risky wall rebounds, precise angle changes, or a powerful launch from a smaller trampoline.
Why It Fits “Bounce Back”
Rebound fits the theme in two ways. Literally, you’re constantly bouncing back from trampolines, walls, and ceilings.
But more importantly, every setback creates an opportunity. A dangerous fall gives you more momentum. A missed route can send you toward a different path. You’re always recovering, redirecting, and finding a new way upward.
The game isn’t about avoiding falls. It’s about using them.
What Makes It Different
A lot of vertical jumping games are about bouncing from platform to platform and trying to climb as high as possible. Rebound is different because you control the direction of every launch.
Instead of just steering while you’re in the air, each trampoline gives you a short aiming window. You choose your angle, think about your speed, and use the tower’s walls and hazards to build your own route upward.
So it feels less like an endless climber and more like a fast precision ricochet game with a clear goal: escape the tower before time runs out.
Core Features
·       Fast 2D trampoline-ricochet movement.
·       An aim-and-launch system where you choose the angle of every rebound.
·       Momentum-based launches, so harder falls create more force.
·       A short slow-motion aiming window after every landing.
·       Walls, ceilings, and hazards that redirect the player.
·       Hand-designed tower sections with safe, risky, and hidden routes.
·       A rising camera and timer to create pressure.
·       Three trampoline types: normal, power, and angle.
·       Quick restarts and a clear escape goal.
Trampoline Types
Normal trampolines give a balanced launch and are used for reliable movement through the tower.
Power trampolines launch you really high, but the extra force makes them harder to control. They’re great for reaching high areas quickly, but risky if you need precision.
Angle trampolines don’t launch you as high, but they let you redirect sharply. They’re important for wall rebounds, tight corridors, and alternate routes.
Visual Style
Rebound has a clean, high-contrast arcade look: a dark industrial tower, glowing trampolines, bright launch arcs, and bold particles whenever you rebound.
When you land, the trampoline squashes. When you launch, it stretches back. The player stretches in the direction they’re launched, and a trail follows their movement. Perfectly aimed rebounds create a burst of particles and a small screen shake.
The visuals are designed to make momentum easy to read. You should instantly understand where you’re going, how fast you’re moving, and which route is risky.
Game Structure
The first version is a single 90-second escape run through a vertical tower. Your goal is to reach the exit at the top before the timer runs out.
The tower has five main sections. Each one introduces a new movement idea: basic trampolines, wall rebounds, moving trampolines, hazards, and a final vertical escape sequence.
There are no enemies, upgrades, collectibles, or multiple levels in the first version. The focus is on making the rebound mechanic feel precise, satisfying, and easy to understand.
Development Plan
I’m building Rebound in Godot as a 2D game. The prototype will focus on the essential systems: player gravity, trampoline collisions, aiming, launch force, wall rebounds, camera pressure, a timer, death, and restart.
Once the core mechanic feels good, I’ll add polish like trampoline squash-and-stretch, launch trails, particles, screen shake, and sound effects.
Controls
A/D or the arrow keys give you slight horizontal movement while you’re in the air.
The mouse aims your rebound direction after you land on a trampoline.
Left click or release launches you.
R restarts the run instantly.
Closing
Rebound is a game about turning every fall into a decision. You fall, you aim, you rebound, and you escape the tower before it collapses.
You don’t just bounce back—you choose how.
